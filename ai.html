01 · Artificial Intelligence

AI in healthcare: what it can do—and what it cannot.

AI can analyze complex datasets, identify patterns and support clinical, research and operational workflows. It should be treated as a tool within evidence-based care, not as a substitute for clinicians or validated medical evaluation. WHO emphasizes safety, equity, ethics and governance in AI for health; the U.S. FDA regulates AI-enabled medical devices through a risk-based medical-device framework. WHO · FDA

Where AI is useful

  • Medical imaging and pattern recognition
  • Clinical decision-support workflows
  • Biomedical research and drug discovery
  • Health-data analysis and risk modeling
  • Public-health surveillance and planning
  • Personalized health and remote monitoring

Questions every user should ask

  • Was the tool validated for this specific use?
  • What population and data were used?
  • How is accuracy and safety monitored?
  • Can bias or missing data affect the result?
  • Who is responsible for the final decision?
  • How are privacy and cybersecurity protected?
Important: An AI-generated health suggestion is not automatically a diagnosis. For symptoms, urgent concerns, medication decisions or abnormal readings, use qualified healthcare professionals and appropriate medical services.

03 · Wearable technology

Your body generates signals. Wearables turn some of them into data.

Wearable devices can include sensors for movement, heart-related signals, temperature and other measurements. Digital health includes wearable devices, mobile health, telehealth and connected technologies. Their meaning depends on device design, measurement quality, context and intended use. 04 · The wearable → AI loop

How a health signal becomes an insight.

1 · SenseSensor captures a physiological or behavioral signal.
2 · ProcessSoftware cleans, organizes and contextualizes the data.
3 · AnalyzeAlgorithms may detect patterns or estimate a metric.
4 · InterpretContext and validated evidence determine what the result means.
5 · ActA person or clinician decides whether any action is appropriate.
6 · LearnFeedback can improve workflows, while governance protects people and data.

06 · Privacy & trust

Health data is powerful—and personal.

Before using a health app or wearable

  • Review what data is collected.
  • Check who can access or share it.
  • Understand retention and deletion options.
  • Use strong account security.
  • Check whether the product is a wellness tool or a regulated medical device.

For AI health tools

  • Look for transparent evidence and intended use.
  • Ask whether validation matches your population.
  • Do not upload sensitive health data unnecessarily.
  • Verify important outputs against authoritative sources.
  • Keep humans in the decision loop for high-stakes care.
